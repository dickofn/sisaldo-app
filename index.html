What it is

Three credit cards, a wallet, and one honest number at the bottom.

Sisaldo tracks what you spend across credit cards and cash, works out what each card really owes, how much room is left on it, and how much money you actually have free once everything you have committed to is taken off. It handles shared credit limits across several cards, and it tracks progress toward the perks a card gives you for using it a certain number of times a month.

It is a personal tool, published so its owner's own devices can update themselves. You are welcome to use it. Nobody is supporting it.

Storage
One SQLite file on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere by default.
Accounts
None. There is no sign-up, and no server that knows you exist.

Platform
Android, sideloaded. Portrait, landscape, and unfolded foldables.

Install

Four steps, and one warning you should read first.

  1. Download the APK

    Use the button at the top of this page, on the phone itself. It always points at the newest release, so there is no version to pick.

  2. Allow the install

    Android will ask whether your browser may install unknown apps. Approve it for that one app. This is the normal sideloading prompt, not a sign anything is wrong.

  3. Tap through Play Protect

    You will see a warning about an unrecognised developer. See the note below for exactly why, then choose to install anyway.

  4. Open it

    The app starts genuinely empty: no accounts, no cards, no sample data. The next section walks through setting it up.

Why Android warns about this app

The APK is signed with a debug key rather than a distribution key. That is deliberate, not an oversight: the key's fingerprint is what the Google sign-in client used for Drive sync is registered against, and changing it would break that. The practical consequence is that Android cannot recognise the publisher, so it warns. If that trade is not one you want to accept, do not install it.

Set it up

Everything on the Dashboard is built from what you enter here.

Sisaldo ships with nothing configured, because the numbers are only worth anything if the cards and limits behind them are yours.

Start with one account

A fresh install has no accounts at all, so the Dashboard has nothing to show and says so. Tap Add an account to begin. An account is either a credit card or cash, and you can have as many of each as you like.

Name it, set its limit, give it a colour

A credit card needs a limit; cash does not have one. The colour is not decoration: it tags that account everywhere it appears, so a glance at the Ledger tells you which card a row belongs to without reading the name.

Leave Limit group on No group unless several cards share one ceiling between them, which the next step covers.

Your accounts, in the order you want them

Everything you add is listed here with its limit. Reorder with the arrows, since that order is the one the Dashboard and the pickers use. Deactivating a card keeps all its history but stops it appearing when you add something new, which is what you want for a card you have closed.

When two cards share one limit

Some banks issue several cards that draw on a single credit line. Adding them up would tell you that you have far more room than you do. A limit group holds the real shared ceiling, and availability is then reported for the group rather than for each card.

In the example, two cards with limits of 25 and 15 million share a ceiling of 30 million, so the group is what gets tracked.

Track a card's perk without counting by hand

Many cards give something back for using them a set number of times, or for spending past a threshold, within each statement period. A bonus rule describes that, and the Dashboard then shows how close you are.

  • Count mode counts qualifying purchases; Total amount mode adds them up.
  • A minimum per-usage amount stops small purchases from counting.
  • Reset day is the day of the month the count starts over, matching your statement.
  • Fees and refunded purchases can be excluded, so the count matches what the bank will actually credit.

Tell it how much money you have right now

This is the one figure Sisaldo cannot work out for itself: what is in your wallet and your bank at this moment. Set it, and everything about what you have left is calculated from there.

You do not need to keep it updated constantly. Set it again whenever it has drifted, typically after payday, and the app applies everything recorded since on top of it.

Day to day

Add a row, read the top of the Dashboard, get on with your life.

Left Over is the number that matters

It is your current money, plus what people owe you, minus everything you owe across every card, including things you have planned but not yet been charged for. The working is printed underneath it so you can see where it came from.

Today is what you have spent since midnight. Card availability shows the room left on each card or shared group, and only counts charges that have actually happened.

Perk progress, and money you have lent

Each bonus rule gets a row of dots and a count, so you can see at a glance whether a perk is still worth chasing this month.

Owed to you gathers anything you paid on someone else's behalf, grouped by person. It is counted in Left Over, because it is money you expect back. Mark one as settled from the transaction itself once they have paid you.

Adding something

Amount, which account, and what it was for. That is the whole required set; everything else has a sensible default.

Kind distinguishes a purchase from a payment to the card, a refund, a cashback, or something you paid on behalf of someone else. Getting the kind right is what lets the app tell "I owe less now" apart from "I spent less".

For a purchase in another currency, set the currency and the rate you were charged at, and the rupiah figure is worked out and kept.

Fixing one that is already there

Tapping any row in the Ledger opens it in a sheet you can correct in place, without leaving the list. Change the amount, move it to another card, or delete it.

The Has a date / No date toggle here is the most consequential control in the app. The next section explains exactly what it changes.

Everything, newest first

The Ledger opens on the current month, because that is almost always what you want, and says how many of your total entries it is showing. Planned entries sit at the top marked Pending, since they have no date to sort by.

The Debit and Credit totals at the bottom are for exactly what is on screen, so they respond to whatever you have filtered or searched.

Narrowing it down

Filter by account, by kind, or by whether an entry is still pending, and set a date range to look at any period rather than this month. Search matches the description.

Because the totals follow the filter, this doubles as a quick way to answer "how much did this card cost me in May".

One card at a time

Per card: what it owes all-time, its progress toward its perk, and a reconciliation note. That last one is a free-text field for what your bank app said and when, so the next time the app and the statement disagree you know when they last agreed.

People, not just amounts

A payee is a name to attach to something you paid for someone else, so "Owed to you" can group by person rather than showing a list of unrelated entries. You can create one straight from the transaction form; this page is for renaming and tidying up.

How the numbers work

One rule explains almost every figure in the app.

A transaction either has a date or it does not, and that single distinction decides which totals it is allowed to affect. An entry with no date is a plan: money you have committed to, that no card has been charged for yet.

Why availability and what-you-owe disagree

They are answering different questions. Your available credit is a fact about the card, so only real charges reduce it. What you owe is a fact about your money, and a bill you have already decided to pay is owed whether or not it has landed. Both figures are correct; expecting them to match is the mistake.

Cash moves no figure at all

Cash accounts always report zero owed, and a cash entry does not change your current money either. That sounds wrong until you remember where current money comes from: you type it in by looking at your wallet, and your wallet already reflects the cash you spent. Applying those entries again would subtract the same rupiah twice. Cash is tracked so you can see where it went, not to move the total.

One consequence worth knowing: cash you lent someone still appears under "Owed to you", but is left out of the amount added back into Left Over, for the same reason.

Nothing is ever stored as a total

Every balance, every availability figure and every rule count is recalculated from your transactions each time it is shown. None of them is saved anywhere. That is slightly more work for the app and the reason it cannot quietly drift out of agreement with itself, which is exactly what the spreadsheets it replaced did.

Backup and sync

Your data is yours, and it can leave whenever you want.

Export a file, or sync to your own Drive

Export backup writes everything to a single file you control, ready for Drive, email, or wherever you keep things. Import backup reads one back.

Importing merges, it does not replace. Importing the same file twice changes nothing, and importing a file from another phone combines the two rather than one overwriting the other.

Google Drive sync is optional and off until you connect it. It uses a hidden folder that only this app can see, so it cannot read anything else in your Drive, and the sign-in token stays in the device's keychain.

It tells you what it is about to do

Before importing, the app says how many entries the file holds, how many are new to this device, and how many records they touch. Nothing happens until you confirm.

One thing that surprises people

App updates only reach installs of the exact same full version. When a new full version comes out, existing installs stop receiving app updates until that version is installed too. So if Settings says you are up to date on app updates, it is still worth installing a new full version promptly when one appears.
